# Observations in the store — field notes, October 2026

Hazel Street Co-op (fictional). Inès, 4 sessions of 2 hours, at the checkout and at the shift desk (the table by the entrance where the shift leader signs people in). Members on shift knew I was there; shoppers did not. No photo, no recording.

Sessions: Tue 2026-10-06 18–20 h · Sat 2026-10-10 10–12 h · Wed 2026-10-14 9–11 h · Sat 2026-10-17 16–18 h.

## Checkout

- 214 checkouts in total. Before each payment the cashier (a member on shift) types the member number on the tablet: "OK" (green), "On alert" (orange), "Suspended" (red).
- 9 members shown "Suspended" (4%). 6 of them were surprised: "Since when?" (Tue), "I did my shift last week!" (Sat 10), "Again?" (Wed).
- 3 of the 9 were let through by the cashier "this time". 6 were not: 4 left the basket at the checkout, 2 asked a friend in the queue to pay.
- Two cashiers gave opposite answers to the same question ("can she pay if she's on alert?"): Tue yes, Sat 17 "I think not".
- The queue waits during these scenes: 3 to 6 minutes each time.
- Sat 10: the member who "did her shift last week" was right: the shift leader showed me the paper sheet with her name. The status on the tablet was not updated yet.

## Shift desk

- Attendance is taken on a paper sheet per shift. The sheets go into a box; a volunteer of the members office types them in "once a week, when I can". The box on 2026-10-14 held sheets back to 2026-10-03.
- Wed 14: 2 members came for a make-up without asking first. One was taken (the team was short), one was sent home.
- A whiteboard "Shifts that need people" by the entrance: 5 to 8 lines each session, names written by hand. On Sat 17, 3 of 6 lines were filled.
- New members ask the shift leader or the cashier "how does it work?" (counted 7 times in 8 hours).

## In passing

- At every session, members talk while they work: the shift is a social moment (jokes, recipes, news of the neighbourhood).
- Sat 17: a shift leader keeps her own notebook "of who swapped with whom", because "the office never knows".
