## Context
What the research of September and October 2026 tells about the shifts of the members of the Hazel Street Co-op (fictional): why they miss them, how they swap them, how they know where they stand. Written by Inès, read out to the team on 2026-10-24 ([[2026-10-24-team-meeting]]).

- From five studies: [[2026-09-member-interviews]], [[2026-09-member-survey]], [[2026-10-store-observations]], [[2026-10-swap-test]], [[2026-10-support-messages]].
- Confidence is Inès's judgment of the support of each insight: strong when heard and measured or seen, with several methods; weak when it rests on a few people or one source.
- What the research does not know yet: [[research-questions]].

## Members miss a shift when their week changes
- Theme: Missing a shift
- Description: A missed shift is a week that moved (a rota, a sick child, a new schedule), rarely a lack of care; members try to swap first.
- Kind: behaviour
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: How might we make a changed week easy to handle before the shift is missed?
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#A shift is missed when the week changes]]
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#A changed schedule is the first reason to miss a shift]]

## A stable week makes a reliable member
- Theme: Missing a shift
- Description: The members who never miss have a week that does not move; they also fill the gaps of others.
- Kind: behaviour
- Confidence: weak
- Implication: Fixed slots work for members with a stable week; keep them, and do not design for them alone.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Members with a stable week never miss]]

## Swaps live in WhatsApp, outside the co-op's tools
- Theme: Swapping
- Description: Members arrange their swaps in the group of their team; the office hears of it late, or never, and a forgotten email turns a swap into a missed shift.
- Kind: behaviour
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: A swap must be as quick as a message in the group, and reach the records by itself.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Swaps are arranged in the team's WhatsApp group]]
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#Most swaps go through WhatsApp]]
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#Swaps are known by the shift leaders, not the office]]

## Members want to give a shift away, not trade it
- Theme: Swapping
- Description: Members do not know who is free; they want to put the shift out for anyone in the co-op to take, not find one person in their team for one exact trade.
- Kind: need
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: Let a member put a shift in a pool open to all members, for anyone to take.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#The team is too small to find a swap]]
  - [[2026-10-swap-test#Choosing a member to swap with blocks the swap]]
  - [[2026-10-swap-test#Members look for "I can't come", not "Swap"]]

## The members office is too slow for a swap
- Theme: Swapping
- Description: The office answers in days; a swap or a change of slot is often answered after the shift, which then counts as missed.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: A swap must not wait for the office.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#The office answers after the shift]]
  - [[2026-10-support-messages#Swaps are announced too late for the office]]
  - [[2026-10-support-messages#Changing slot takes several emails]]

## Members do not know where they stand
- Theme: Status
- Description: Half of the members do not know how many make-up shifts they owe, and "On alert" means nothing to them; they want a number.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: Show the number of make-up shifts owed, in plain words.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Nobody knows how many make-ups they owe]]
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#Half of the members do not know their status]]
  - [[2026-10-swap-test#"On alert" is not understood]]

## Members learn they are suspended at the checkout
- Theme: Status
- Description: The first sign of a suspension is the red screen at the checkout, with a full basket and a queue behind: a moment of shame, and minutes lost for everyone.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: How might we tell members of a suspension before they come to shop?
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Suspension is learned at the checkout]]
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#One member in seven was stopped at the checkout]]
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#Suspended members are stopped at the checkout, surprised]]

## The status is wrong for days after a shift
- Theme: Status
- Description: Attendance goes from a paper sheet to the system once a week; until then, a member who did the shift can be shown suspended.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: A shift done must reach the status the same day.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#The status lags behind the shifts done]]
  - [[2026-10-support-messages#Members suspended for a shift they did]]

## Cashiers decide alone whether a suspended member pays
- Theme: Status
- Description: There is no shared rule at the checkout: one cashier lets a suspended member pay, another does not.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: weak
- Implication: A rule for the co-op to set, not for the app to guess.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#Cashiers apply the rule differently]]

## Open shifts would be filled if members saw them
- Theme: Make-up shifts
- Description: Members with free time at short notice would fill a gap or do a make-up if they knew where a team is short; the whiteboard fills half of its gaps, and a list of open shifts was understood at once.
- Kind: need
- Confidence: moderate
- Implication: Show the open shifts of the whole co-op, with the spots left, where members look.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Some members want shifts at short notice]]
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#The whiteboard fills only half of the gaps]]
  - [[2026-10-swap-test#The list of open shifts works]]

## New members leave the onboarding without the rules
- Theme: Joining
- Description: The onboarding session gives "3 hours every 4 weeks", not the make-up rule nor where to find a shift; the cashier explains it later, in the store.
- Kind: pain
- Confidence: moderate
- Implication: How might we teach the make-up rule when it first matters, not at the session?
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#New members leave the onboarding without the rules]]
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#New members ask how it works in the store]]

## Working together is what members value most
- Theme: What members value
- Description: Members love the co-op and dislike the organisation of the shifts; what they value is the shift itself, with people they would not meet otherwise.
- Kind: gain
- Confidence: strong
- Implication: The app arranges the shifts; it does not replace being together in the store.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#Working together is what members value]]
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#Members like the co-op, not the shifts]]
  - [[2026-10-store-observations#The shift is a social moment]]

## Not every member will use a phone app
- Theme: What members value
- Description: One member in eight would not use an app, most of them 65 or older; the oldest participant of the test gave up on the list of open shifts.
- Kind: need
- Confidence: moderate
- Implication: Keep the whiteboard and the shift desk next to the app.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-09-member-survey#Most members would use an app, not all]]
  - [[2026-10-swap-test#The list of open shifts works]]

## Life events need a pause, not a suspension
- Theme: What members value
- Description: A birth, a long trip or an illness stops the shifts for weeks; with no pause in the rules, the office decides case by case, and some members get suspended or think of leaving.
- Kind: need
- Confidence: moderate
- Implication: A pause for the co-op to define; the app shows it once it exists.
- Supported by:
  - [[2026-10-support-messages#Members ask for a pause, and get suspended]]
  - [[2026-09-member-interviews#The rules push some members out]]

## Open questions
- Why do so few members name "no swap found" as the reason for a missed shift? 9% in the survey ([[2026-09-member-survey#A changed schedule is the first reason to miss a shift]]), while the interviews and the test show the swap as the problem ([[#Members want to give a shift away, not trade it]]).
  - Assumption: Q5 allowed one answer: a member whose week changed and who found no swap answered "schedule changed".
- Do members leave because of the shifts? A former board member says 3 of the 4 he knows ([[2026-09-member-interviews#The rules push some members out]]); the office has no reason for 160 of the 214 who left in a year ([[2026-10-support-messages#One member in fourteen is suspended]]). Not an insight yet: no member who left was heard.
